What a kidney stone feels like

A kidney stone is a hard deposit that forms in the kidney and causes trouble when it moves into the urinary tract. The pain can be intense and sudden. The good news is that nearly every stone can be treated, and many never need a procedure at all.

Sudden, severe pain in the back or side
Pain that comes in waves toward the groin
Blood in the urine
Nausea or vomiting
A frequent, urgent need to urinate
Pain or burning when you go

The right treatment for the stone

The best approach depends on the stone's size, location, and makeup. We will confirm which one fits and get you comfortable as quickly as possible.

Small stones · pass naturally

Watchful waiting

Small stones, under about 5 mm, often pass on their own. With extra hydration and medication to ease the way, Dr. Sawkar monitors your progress so nothing is missed.

  • No procedure needed
  • Hydration and medication to help
  • Monitored until it passes
Non-invasive · no incisions

Shockwave lithotripsy

Focused shockwaves break the stone into fragments from outside the body, with no incisions, using sedation for comfort. The pieces then pass naturally over the days and weeks that follow.

  • No incisions or cutting
  • Effective for stones up to about 2 cm
  • Outpatient, home the same day
Any location · laser

Laser ureteroscopy

A slim scope is passed through the urethra and bladder to reach the stone, where a Holmium laser breaks or vaporizes it. No incisions, and highly effective anywhere in the urinary tract.

  • No incisions
  • Works on harder and larger stones
  • Outpatient, home the same day
Long term · stay stone-free

Prevention

Once the stone is handled, the goal is preventing the next one. A metabolic review and a simple, personalized plan can meaningfully lower your odds of forming another.

  • Metabolic and dietary review
  • A plan built around your stone type
  • Fewer recurrences over time

Kidney stone treatment by size

Size and location decide most of the plan. The millimeters on your CT report place you in one of these rows; pain, infection, and how well the kidney is draining decide how quickly we move, and they outrank the number.

Kidney stone care in Orange County

Same-day and next-day visits for acute stone pain are seen at the Santa Ana office at 720 N Tustin Ave. If you already have a CT or ultrasound from an emergency room or urgent care, bring the disc or have the report sent; the images are reviewed at the visit rather than at a later appointment. The first job is bringing the pain under control. The second is a plan you leave with, whether that is a trial of passage with a strainer and tamsulosin or a date for a procedure.

Procedures are done as outpatient surgery at Providence St. Joseph Hospital in Orange, where Dr. Sawkar is Chief of Urology. If you have already been to an emergency room with a stone, or had a stent placed there to relieve a blockage, the office arranges definitive treatment and stent removal within days rather than weeks, so you are not living with a stent while you wait for a first appointment. Medicare and most PPO plans are accepted, and follow-up after the stone is cleared can be done by telehealth when that is easier.

Common questions about kidney stone treatment

How quickly can I be seen for a kidney stone in Orange County

Same-day or next-day for acute stone pain. Send the office a secure message or call; bring any imaging or emergency-room records, and the visit covers pain control, a review of the scan, and the plan.

Will my kidney stone pass on its own

Size and location decide. Stones under 5 mm pass most of the time, 5 to 10 mm stones pass about half the time, and stones over 10 mm rarely do. The kidney stone size chart on this site walks through the odds and timing.

Which is better, shockwave lithotripsy or ureteroscopy

Ureteroscopy clears more stones in one session and works on hard or lower-pole stones; shockwave avoids any scope and suits softer stones in the kidney or upper ureter. Stone density on CT, location, and your preferences decide.

Do I need a stent after ureteroscopy

Often, for a few days to about a week, to keep the ureter open while swelling settles. It is removed in the office, usually without another anesthetic. Not every case needs one.

Is kidney stone surgery outpatient

Shockwave lithotripsy and ureteroscopy are outpatient; you go home the same day. Percutaneous nephrolithotomy for very large stones usually means one night in the hospital.

How do I prevent another kidney stone

Enough fluid to make about 2.5 liters of urine a day, less sodium, normal dietary calcium rather than restriction, and citrate from lemons or medication when needed. A 24-hour urine collection after the first stone tailors the plan to your chemistry.
