Side by side

Both remove obstructing tissue through the urethra. The difference is how: TURP carves with a heated electric loop, guided by eye; Aquablation resects with a heat-free waterjet executing a robotic plan built on live ultrasound.

The bottom line

If you have been told you need TURP, you owe yourself one conversation before scheduling it. For most surgical candidates the modern trade favors Aquablation: the same relief, dramatically better odds of preserving ejaculation, and results that do not depend on how steady one surgeon's hand is on one afternoon. And if the real goal is avoiding the operating room entirely, PAE treats the same problem without surgery at all, which is a different comparison worth reading.

Common questions

Is Aquablation better than TURP

In the randomized WATER trial, symptom relief was equivalent, while ejaculatory dysfunction was several-fold lower after Aquablation. For large prostates, Aquablation outperformed where TURP struggles. TURP remains a proven benchmark, but for most candidates the modern trade favors the robot.

Why do surgeons still do TURP

It has a seventy-year track record, works well in experienced hands, and is available everywhere, while Aquablation requires a robotic system and specific surgeon training. Where both are available, the choice usually comes down to the sexual-function difference and gland size.

Is the recovery different

Broadly similar: both involve a hospital stay, a catheter for a day or two, and one to two weeks back to routine. The meaningful differences are during and after: less bleeding risk with the heat-free waterjet in trials, and far better preservation of ejaculation.

Does insurance cover Aquablation like TURP

Yes. Aquablation is typically covered by Medicare and most PPO and commercial plans the same way TURP is, when BPH symptoms are documented. The office verifies your specific coverage before anything is scheduled.
