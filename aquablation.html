How Aquablation works

Aquablation pairs real-time ultrasound imaging with a computer-controlled robotic arm to map and remove the obstructing prostate tissue using a high-velocity saline waterjet. Because the waterjet uses no heat, the risk of thermal injury to the surrounding nerves and sphincters is minimized.

The robotic system carries out the plan with sub-millimeter accuracy every time, regardless of prostate size or complexity, which makes it especially well suited to large or complex prostates. Dr. Sawkar has performed more than 250 Aquablation procedures, the highest volume of any surgeon in Orange County and among the highest on the West Coast.

Aquablation was tested the hard way: head-to-head against TURP, the century-old surgical benchmark, in the randomized WATER trial, with a companion WATER II study dedicated to large 80 to 150 cc glands.

Risks, downsides and success rate, honestly

Every real treatment has a real downside, and a surgeon 250 cases in has seen each of these. The success side first: in the randomized WATER trial, symptom scores fell by roughly 15 points and stayed there through five years, and about 1 in 20 men needed a repeat procedure in that time, fewer than in the TURP arm. Here is the other side of the ledger.

Bleeding

The most discussed downside. Blood in the urine for one to three weeks is expected. Bleeding heavy enough to need a transfusion ran a few percent in the early large-prostate series and is uncommon now that the resection bed is cauterized at the end of the procedure.

Ejaculation

About 1 in 10 men in the trial lost ejaculation, versus roughly 1 in 3 after TURP. The heat-free waterjet and a plan that spares the ejaculatory ducts are why the number is low; it is not zero.

Erections

No meaningful change on average in the trials. Men who arrive with erectile trouble usually leave with the same erectile trouble, no better and no worse, and men with good function keep it.

Leakage

Urgency and small leaks are common in the first weeks while the resection surface heals and typically settle by three months. Persistent stress incontinence is rare, in the low single digits or less.

The catheter

Most men have it out in a day or two. A minority need it for several more days, usually men with very large glands or a weak bladder going in; it comes out once you are emptying well.

The rest of the list

Urinary infection, a scar narrowing the urethra or bladder neck that needs a minor procedure (a few percent), and the risks of anesthesia and a hospital night. Men who cannot pause blood thinners, or whose prostate exceeds about 150 cc, usually look at PAE instead.

If you have searched for Aquablation problems or complications, this is the list. Nothing here is hidden at the consultation either; the point of a candid conversation is that you choose with the whole picture.

Recovery, in plain terms

Expect one night in the hospital and a catheter for a day or two. Some blood in the urine during the first days is normal and clears. Desk work resumes within about a week for most men, with heavy lifting and hard exercise on hold for two to four weeks while the prostate heals from the inside. Flow improvement is typically obvious as soon as the catheter comes out, and it is durable, because the obstructing tissue is gone rather than pushed aside or shrunk. Common questions about Aquablation

Is Aquablation real surgery

Yes. It is a hospital procedure under spinal or general anesthesia in which obstructing tissue is physically removed. What sets it apart from traditional surgery is how: a heat-free waterjet executing a robotic plan built from live ultrasound, designed to protect continence and sexual function.

How is Aquablation different from TURP

In the randomized WATER trial, symptom relief matched TURP while ejaculatory dysfunction was several-fold lower. The robot also makes results consistent across prostate sizes and shapes, including large glands where TURP becomes slow and less complete.

Is my prostate too large for Aquablation

Rarely. It is proven from 30 to 150 cc, a wider range than almost any other option. Beyond roughly 150 cc, PAE, which has no practical size ceiling, usually enters the conversation, and Dr. Sawkar offers both.

Will it affect ejaculation or erections

Far less than traditional surgery. Heat-free resection and robotic planning spare the structures involved, and most men preserve ejaculation, a several-fold improvement over TURP in trial data. Erections are typically unaffected.

Does the surgeon's volume matter

Yes. The robot executes the plan, but the surgeon builds it, and planning judgment comes from repetition. Dr. Sawkar has performed more than 250 Aquablation procedures at Providence St. Joseph Hospital, the highest volume in Orange County and among the highest on the West Coast.

What are the downsides of Aquablation

A hospital procedure under anesthesia with a night's stay, a catheter for a day or two, blood in the urine for a few weeks, and a small chance of bleeding that needs treatment. About 1 in 10 men lose ejaculation, far fewer than after TURP. Men who cannot stop blood thinners or have a prostate over about 150 cc usually look at PAE instead.

What is the success rate of Aquablation

In the randomized WATER trial, symptom scores fell by roughly 15 points, relief held through five years, and about 1 in 20 men needed a repeat procedure in that time, fewer than with TURP. Results in large glands of 80 to 150 cc were as strong in the companion WATER II study.

Does Medicare cover Aquablation

Yes. Medicare covers Aquablation on the same medical-necessity basis as TURP, and most PPO and commercial plans follow. Your share depends on your plan's structure, and the office verifies coverage before anything is scheduled. The cost page walks through each situation.

How much does Aquablation cost

With Original Medicare, expect the $283 Part B deductible if it is not yet met, then 20 percent of the Medicare-allowed amount for the surgeon and anesthesia, with the hospital share under Part A or Part B depending on how the stay is classified. A Medigap supplement usually absorbs the coinsurance. PPO plans apply their deductible and coinsurance, and self-pay patients get one bundled quote.
